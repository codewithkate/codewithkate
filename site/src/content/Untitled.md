**Design:** A between-subjects online experiment (pre-registered, large-N via Prolific or similar platform) using a scholarship selection task modeled on Iyengar & Westwood's Study 2, but with a critical extension.

Participants evaluate paired high school applicants where one is an in-partisan and the other an out-partisan (cued via extracurricular membership, as in the original). The key manipulation is the **verifiability of the academic credential**:

- **Condition 1 — Subjective/Unverifiable Merit:** Applicants differ on a recommendation letter excerpt describing one as "exceptionally driven" (the out-partisan is described more favorably). This is opinion-based and inherently ambiguous—exactly the kind of information partisan heuristics should easily override.
    
- **Condition 2 — Objective but Ambiguous Merit:** Applicants differ on GPA (3.5 vs. 4.0, as in the original Iyengar & Westwood design). This replicates the baseline and provides an anchor.
    
- **Condition 3 — Objective and Independently Verified Merit:** The higher-GPA out-partisan applicant's transcript is described as "independently audited and confirmed by the National Student Clearinghouse." This adds an institutional verification layer that makes dismissing the information cognitively costlier.
    
- **Condition 4 — Objective, Verified, and Consequential:** Same as Condition 3, but participants are told their choice will be entered into a pool where a real $50 scholarship will actually be awarded to a student matching the selected profile's characteristics, introducing real stakes for ignoring merit.
    

All conditions cross partisan match/mismatch with whether the more-qualified candidate is the in-partisan or out-partisan, yielding a clean 4 (verifiability) × 2 (which candidate is more qualified) × 2 (partisan match) factorial.

**Primary DV:** Which candidate is selected for the scholarship.

**Secondary DVs:** Confidence in one's choice (to measure whether partisan override operates with or without subjective certainty); time spent on the decision (to capture deliberation consistent with Carlin & Love's dual-process framework); and a post-decision justification open-text response coded for whether participants cite merit vs. non-merit reasons.
